What happens when a roofing call is missed

  1. A homeowner calls about an active leak, shingles in the yard after a storm, or an inspection for an insurance claim. Your crews are on roofs and the call rings out.

  2. Under a minute later, their phone buzzes: a text that says you got the call and asks what is happening at the house.

  3. Their reply lands in one inbox, flagged urgent if water is actively coming in, so you call back the right job first.

Sample moment

Sample: Thursday, 9:10 PM, an hour after a storm front moved through. Water is coming through a kitchen ceiling. The owner is across town finishing an emergency tarp job and misses the call. The customer gets a text in under a minute: “Sorry we missed you — reply with what's happening at the house and we'll get you on the board.” She replies “dripping through the light fixture, got a bucket under it,” and the lead lands in the owner's inbox flagged “Emergency — active leak.” He calls back before he leaves the driveway, gets a crew out to tarp it in the morning, and the customer stops calling down the list.

Why the first reply matters

If you advertise on Google Local Services Ads, a missed call can now cost you money on top of the lost job. Since October 1, 2026, Google can charge a missed call during your posted hours as a lead when the caller stays on the line over 20 seconds (the timer starts after a phone-menu keypress). You pay for the lead and the conversation never happened. A text back inside a minute is what turns that call into a conversation instead.

Roofing calls bunch up. The two days after a storm are when the phone rings nonstop — leaks, missing shingles, insurance questions — and that is exactly when every crew is on a roof and nobody is near the phone.

The LSA rule is Google's, as announced. Neither point above is a count of your company's calls. Your missed calls are the number that counts. The math below uses your numbers, not ours.

Questions roofers ask

What does a customer get when I miss their call?

A text back in under a minute, day or night. It says you got their call and asks what they need. Their reply lands in one inbox, and urgent jobs are flagged first.

We have someone in the office answering. Is this still useful?

Only for the hours nobody is in the office, and for the two days after a storm, when every line rings at once and the office cannot keep up. Nights and weekends count too. If every call already gets picked up right away, you do not need this.

Does it answer my phone live?

No. It follows up by text after a call is missed. If you want a voice to pick up the call itself, see the AI Phone Receptionist from Northbeam Builds.

What does it cost a roofing company, in total?

How long does setup take?
